# Graphing.Symbolics provenance

`Graphing.Symbolics` is an independent implementation written for this repository. It contains no copied CAS source, generated third-party source, binary dependency, or external package reference. Its exact integer and rational kernel uses the calculator's existing RatPak `NUMBER` primitives through a source project reference to `CalcManagerPort`; the integration keeps immutable canonical snapshots and does not modify RatPak. The remaining infrastructure uses .NET BCL types and immutable collections.

The following publications are specifications for theorem selection and independently derived algorithms, not sources of implementation text:

- Daniel Richardson, “Some undecidable problems involving elementary functions of a real variable” — the reason every obligation has a proved/disproved/unknown result and unsupported elementary-function questions remain unknown.
- David Stoutemyer, “Simplifying Fractions of Powers” — domain-preserving rewrite requirements. The value DAG and definedness formula are deliberately separate, so cancellation and zero-product rewrites cannot erase holes.
- Basu, Pollack, and Roy, *Algorithms in Real Algebraic Geometry* — polynomial remainder sequences, Sturm sign determination, square-free decomposition, algebraic isolating intervals, and cell decomposition.
- Even quartic ranges of the form `a x^4+b x^2+c` are reduced exactly to the quadratic `a u^2+b u+c` on the closed ray `u=x^2 >= 0`. This proves whether the global endpoint is the ray endpoint `c` or the rational vertex image `c-b^2/(4a)` without approximating its generally irrational `x` witnesses; certificate replay repeats the reduction independently and rechecks all coefficient limits.
- Daniel Perrucci and Marie-Françoise Roy, “Elementary recursive quantifier elimination based on Thom encoding” — derivative-sign (Thom) encodings attached to isolated algebraic roots.
- Chen, Ge, Li, and Xia, “Real root isolation of regular trigonometric polynomials” and the associated ISSAC 2023 decision-procedure slides — tangent half-angle reduction, projective endpoint handling, and periodic root families. No LGPL implementation was inspected or copied.
- Integer harmonics are reduced independently with the elementary angle-addition recurrences `sin(a+b) = sin(a)cos(b) + cos(a)sin(b)` and `cos(a+b) = cos(a)cos(b) − sin(a)sin(b)`, evaluated by binary exponentiation over the exact tangent-half-angle rational functions. The parallel finite Fourier representation records the same integral frequency, so parity and the least period are checked independently of root isolation.
- A total real trigonometric polynomial with exactly one nonzero Fourier frequency is reconstructed as `c + a cos(kx) + b sin(kx)`. The elementary phase-amplitude identity gives the exact closed image `c + [-sqrt(a^2+b^2), sqrt(a^2+b^2)]`; replay independently rebuilds the conjugate Fourier pair, rejects every additional harmonic or retained domain guard, and recomputes the squared radius under the deterministic coefficient budget.
- A total real trigonometric polynomial whose only positive Fourier frequencies are `k` and `2k` is admitted to the quadratic-harmonic range theorem only when its conjugate coefficients reconstruct exactly as `a sin(kx)^2+b sin(kx)+c` or `a cos(kx)^2+b cos(kx)+c`, with nonzero rational `a` and `b`. Since sine and cosine each map the real line onto `[-1,1]`, the exact range is obtained by comparing the quadratic's two endpoint values and its rational vertex when that vertex lies in the interval. Replay rebuilds the Fourier coefficients, verifies total definedness and the conjugate/alignment conditions, and recomputes the complete closed image without sampling.
- Cooper’s Presburger elimination theorem — integer-preimage obligations and the primitive inverse metadata used for `IsInteger(tan(x))`.
- The reciprocal trigonometric identities `cot(u) = cos(u)/sin(u)`, `sec(u) = 1/cos(u)`, and `csc(u) = 1/sin(u)` are applied only with the denominator-nonzero guard retained in the semantic DAG. Affine range, critical-point, pole, monotonicity, parity, and least-period claims are independently derived from the exact sine/cosine sign cells. For a nonzero outer shift, zero obligations reduce exactly to the guarded equations `cos(u) = -A/c`, `sin(u) = -A/c`, or `tan(u) = -A/c`; exact coefficient ordering decides bounded inverse targets, and the replay checker reconstructs the guarded ratio, target, angle unit, and periodic families before accepting the claim.
- Ordered exact-coefficient claims use only direct affine, quadratic, linear-fractional, and affine sine/cosine/tangent identities. Coefficient signs are established by rational interval enclosures; roots, vertices, determinant signs, pole exclusions, inverse images, critical families, and fundamental periods are then derived symbolically. The production checker independently re-extracts the domain-preserving pattern and replays every enclosure premise before instantiating the claimed theorem. Failure to prove an exact comparison remains unknown.
- A linear-fractional exact-coefficient expression whose determinant is proved zero is constant on every connected component of its retained denominator-nonzero domain. Cross multiplication gives `N(x) = k D(x)` exactly; when the checked scalar `k` is nonzero this also proves the zero set empty without comparing syntactically different representations of the common numerator and denominator root. The retained hole still controls parity, periodicity, and the split into maximal monotonicity components, and certificate replay rechecks the zero determinant and all exact-scalar order witnesses.
- For a reduced rational function `c/D(x)` with nonzero rational constant `c`, positive odd-degree `D`, and domain exactly `D(x) != 0`, the range is `R \ {0}`. Zero is impossible on-domain. For every nonzero `y`, the polynomial `D(x)-c/y` retains the odd degree and therefore has opposite signs on its two infinite tails, so it has a real root; at that root `D(x)=c/y != 0` and the function value is `y`. The dedicated production certificate binds the original definedness formula, reduced numerator and denominator, and a replayed cell decomposition of the denominator-nonzero domain. Extra holes, even degrees, nonconstant numerators, altered cells, and altered claims are rejected.
- For a radian affine tangent `A tan(a x+b)+c` with nonzero rational `b`, the pole lattice is `(pi/2-b)/a + (pi/a) Z`. Reflection invariance of that lattice would require `2b/pi` to be an integer, which is impossible by the irrationality of pi. This proves parity is neither without sampling. Origin substitution uses the exact odd identity `tan(-b)=-tan(b)` and retains `A tan(b)+c` as a symbolic real value; exact quarter-turn phases additionally use `tan(π/4)=1`, `tan(−π/4)=−1`, and the half-turn pole cases. Certificate replay independently re-extracts the guarded tangent pattern, selected angle unit, exact coefficient evidence, and these claims.
- Absolute-value composition claims use only the identities `sqrt(g²) = |g|`, `cos(|u|) = cos(u)`, and `cosh(|u|) = cosh(u)`, together with exact affine pullbacks of primitive zero, derivative-sign, and convexity cells. The theorem is admitted only when replay proves the original semantic domain is all real, so simplification cannot erase a retained hole; cusp points and constrained periodic families are checked explicitly.
- Fixed nonintegral rational powers use the locked principal-real convention directly: for reduced `p/q`, the rational base is constrained to be nonnegative when `p > 0` and positive when `p < 0`, regardless of whether `q` is odd. On that guarded domain, injectivity and the identities `d(r^(p/q))/dx = (p/q)r^(p/q-1)r'` and `sign(d²(r^(p/q))/dx²) = sign(p) sign(q r r'' + (p-q)(r')²)` reduce parity, zeros, strict extrema, monotonicity, and genuine curvature changes to exact rational sign cells. Range fibers clear denominators only after retaining the domain formula; finite limits and linear-growth cases are derived from rational leading coefficients. Fixed even `root(r,n)` is admitted through the same proof only as `r^(1/n)`. The checker rebuilds the sign chart and every range fiber, so altered exponents, holes, cells, or witnesses are rejected.
- Guarded constants over a periodic puncture lattice are derived without filling any hole. A single open interval whose width equals its positive translation period is exactly the complement of one arithmetic lattice; reflection symmetry is decided by the exact condition `2a/P ∈ ℤ` for puncture offset `a` and period `P`. Constant range, zero set, intercept, strict-extrema, affine-convexity, limit, monotonicity, and least-domain-period claims then follow directly. The checker reconstructs the original definedness formula and periodic domain before replaying any claim.
- Unary-composition claims use guarded inverse identities and exact pullbacks of affine sine, tangent, and absolute-value cells. Principal-branch compositions such as `asin(sin(u))`, `acos(cos(u))`, and `atan(tan(u))` retain their branch endpoints and tangent holes; `exp(sin(u))`, `sqrt(sin(u))`, `ln(sin(u))`, `ln(abs(u))`, and `log10(abs(u))` derive range, extrema, curvature, poles, and monotonicity directly from exact derivative signs. Natural and common logarithms remain distinct in the extracted pattern, intercept value, and certificate; exact rational powers of ten are reduced only through charged integer division. Common-logarithm sine compositions route to the separate base-aware affine-phase proof. The checker re-extracts the nested pattern and requires the original definedness formula to match before replay. Transcendental curvature equations with unisolated roots, including those for `tan(sin(u))` and `cos(sin(u))`, remain unknown.
- Affine-phase sine compositions `sqrt(sin(a x+b))`, `ln(sin(a x+b))`, and `log10(sin(a x+b))` are admitted when `a` is a nonzero rational and `b` is an exact affine combination of pi and a rational constant in the selected angle unit. Exact sine sign cells give the closed or open periodic domain, zeros, endpoint extrema, logarithmic poles, monotonicity cells, parity, and least period. On every positive cell, `d2(sqrt(s))/dx2 = -a^2(1+s^2)/(4 s^(3/2)) < 0`, while either logarithm has second derivative equal to a positive base-dependent scale times `-a^2 csc^2(a x+b) < 0`; hence neither family has an inflection point. Replay re-extracts the original partial-function definedness, the angle unit, normalized phase, and every exact result, so a changed unit, phase, guard, or claim is rejected.
- Base-ten logarithms of exact affine squares are derived directly from `log10((a x+b)^2) = 2 log10|a x+b|` on the guarded domain `a x+b != 0`; this identity is never used to fill the excluded affine root. The two zero fibers are the exact pullbacks of `a x+b = +/-1`, while `f'(x) = 2/(ln(10)(x+b/a))` and `f''(x) = -2/(ln(10)(x+b/a)^2)` establish the two maximal monotonicity cells, absence of strict extrema and inflections, and the unique vertical asymptote. The dedicated checker re-extracts the affine square, re-solves the original definedness formula, and recomputes every claim.
- Affine sign claims use the exact piecewise definition `sgn(u) = -1, 0, 1` according as `u < 0`, `u = 0`, or `u > 0`. For an everywhere-defined exact affine argument `u = a x+b`, coefficient signs and the unique zero fiber are established by the exact-scalar kernel; range, parity, one-sided tail limits, empty strict-extrema and curvature sets, monotone orientation, and periodicity then follow directly from those three cells. Partial or nonlinear arguments are rejected. The dedicated checker independently re-extracts the original affine argument and its definedness formula before recomputing every claim.
- Zero-base affine powers follow the locked real-power convention directly: `0^(a x+b)` is defined exactly where `a x+b > 0`, and equals zero throughout that strict half-line. Coefficients may be rational or supported exact symbolic scalars such as `pi`, `e`, radicals, and their proved combinations. The exact-scalar kernel establishes every coefficient sign by structural identities or outward rational enclosures; if it cannot order a coefficient, the obligation remains unknown. The nonzero affine slope then orients the strict half-line at the exact threshold `-b/a`; this does not route symbolic inequalities through the rational-only CAD converter. The resulting domain gives the range, zero set, optional origin value, empty strict-extrema and curvature sets, constant monotonicity cell, zero tail limit, and nonperiodicity. Any hole in the base or exponent, a nonlinear exponent, or a constant exponent is rejected by this specialized theorem. Production replay does not call the analyzer's context factory or theorem dispatcher: it independently rematches both original operands, re-extracts and orders the coefficients, rechecks the literal strict-positive definedness formula required by `0^e`, reconstructs the threshold, and recomputes every claim under the same deterministic work and coefficient limits.
- Centered zero-base tangent powers follow the same convention: `0^(A tan(a x))` is defined exactly on the open tangent-sign cells where `A tan(a x) > 0`, and is identically zero there. For nonzero exact `A` and nonzero rational `a`, tangent's exact half-period sign table proves the periodic domain/zero family, empty strict-extrema and curvature sets, constant cell family, zero horizontal limit, asymmetric-domain parity, and least period. Shifted tangents, retained holes, and inexact coefficients remain outside this theorem. Replay reconstructs the original power definedness and sign-cell family before recomputing every claim.
- Guarded cotangent identities reduce an everywhere-defined exact Pythagorean numerator over a centered affine tangent to a scaled cotangent value while retaining every original tangent hole. The source domain therefore excludes both sine-zero division points and cosine-zero tangent poles, not merely the simplified cotangent's poles. Exact tangent/cotangent sign tables prove the punctured range, odd parity, true vertical poles, monotonicity cells, and least period. Replay re-reduces the original numerator, re-extracts the denominator pattern, and reconstructs the stricter division definedness before recomputing each claim.
- Centered affine sine multiplied by the guarded identity `x/x` retains the source hole at the origin even though its value term reduces to the sine. The ordinary sine range and extrema survive because their witnesses avoid the hole; the zero and inflection lattices explicitly exclude the missing origin, and the monotonicity family splits the affected central cell. A single retained hole destroys every nonzero translation symmetry, so this function is proved nonperiodic rather than inheriting sine's value-only period. Replay requires the original self-division and multiplicative-identity rewrite provenance and independently reconstructs all guarded claims.
- Bounded radical-tangent products of the exact source form `sqrt(x) * sqrt(c-x) * tan(x)` are admitted in radians for rational `0 < c <= 1`. Here `c` may be a rational literal or a source expression built solely from exactly checked rational negation, addition, subtraction, multiplication, and nonzero division. Every arithmetic node is re-evaluated from its source operands, its exact constant-fold rewrite and true guard are replayed, coefficient limits are charged, and its definedness, continuity, and differentiability formulas must be inherited exactly; a folded value with a hidden source-domain hole therefore remains inadmissible. The classical exact bound `π > 2` gives `[0,c]` as a subset of `[0,1]`, which lies strictly before the first positive tangent pole at `π/2`; consequently the two radical guards reduce the domain exactly to `[0,c]`, tangent is finite and positive in its interior, and the only zeros are its endpoints. Continuity on this compact domain excludes vertical asymptotes, while boundedness of the domain excludes infinite-tail asymptotes; the nonsymmetric bounded domain is neither even nor odd and cannot be invariant under a nonzero period. Range, extrema, inflections, and monotonicity are intentionally left unknown because they require a certified interior analytic critical-point argument. Replay reconstructs the exact multiplication tree, the rational endpoint's complete arithmetic source shape, all three original source factors, every definedness/regularity guard, the rational endpoint bound, and each published claim; value-equivalent unsafe rewrites and retained holes are rejected.
- Rational affine floor claims are derived from the exact cells `n <= a x+b < n+1`. Each integer value therefore has a maximal half-open preimage whose orientation is determined by the sign of `a`; the zero cell, origin value, integer range, empty strict-extrema and curvature sets, constant cell family, asymptotes, parity, and nonperiodicity follow without sampling. Arguments with retained holes, nonlinear terms, or nonrational coefficients remain outside this theorem. The dedicated checker re-extracts the original total affine argument and independently rebuilds every half-open cell and claim.
- Lower and upper envelopes of two total rational-affine branches are derived by exact slope comparison and, for nonparallel branches, their unique rational intersection. Each side of that intersection is an affine tail, so exact image and zero sets, strict kink extrema, parity, maximal monotonicity regions, and horizontal or oblique tail asymptotes follow directly; parallel branches reduce to the selected affine line. Operands with retained holes are rejected even when value cancellation makes a branch look affine. Replay re-extracts both original operands, rechecks all rational-coefficient limits and definedness formulas, and recomputes the envelope without trusting the stored branch order, intersection, or claim.
- Elementary pullback claims use the monotone substitutions `t = sqrt(x)`, `t = ln(x)`, `t = log10(x)`, `t = exp(x)`, and the branchwise bijection `t = tan(x)`. Sine zeros, extrema, derivative-sign cells, domain holes, least periods, and limits are pulled back as exact integer families. Natural and common logarithms are separate certificate kinds: `sin(ln(x))` curvature follows from `sin(t) + cos(t) = 0`, whereas `sin(log10(x))` has `f''(x) = -(sin(t) + ln(10) cos(t))/(x² ln(10)²)`, giving the exact two families `t = 2πn-arctan(ln(10))` and `t = 2πn+π-arctan(ln(10))`. Replay re-extracts the original logarithm name and rejects a base-swapped certificate. The identities `d(exp(x)+x)/dx = exp(x)+1`, `d²(exp(x)+x)/dx² = exp(x)`, and `exp(x)+x = 0 iff x = -W(1)` prove its unique zero, strict increase, convexity, and left oblique tail. Curvature equations such as `t sin(t)+cos(t)=0`, `cos(t)-t sin(t)=0`, and `2t cos(t)-(1+t²)sin(t)=0` remain unknown until the validated analytic backend can issue complete isolated-root-family certificates.
- Strictly monotone Puiseux-polynomial sine phases are handled as one algebraic class rather than a portfolio of expression shapes. Rational-power arithmetic preserves every principal-branch requirement, while an explicit odd `root` retains its signed real branch. Only an all-real domain or the exact closed ray `x >= 0` is admitted. The bijective coordinate `x=t^L` lowers the finite rational-exponent phase to an ordinary rational polynomial `P(t)`, where `L` is the checked least common multiple of exponent denominators; on all reals every denominator must be odd, so the coordinate remains a signed bijection. Exact cell decomposition proves that `P'` never has the wrong sign. Sine zeros and extrema are then structured preimages of its integer angle lattices; the same argument proves the full range, endpoint extrema, derivative-sign monotonicity sets, and absence of asymptotes. A full-real affine phase gets the exact scaled sine period; every admitted ray phase and every nonlinear full-real phase is nonperiodic. Full-real admission additionally requires the lowered polynomial to have odd degree. Curvature remains unknown. Replay independently rebuilds the phase, retained domain, lowering, derivative-violation cells, angle-unit targets, and each claim; unsafe identities such as `sqrt(x^6)=x^3` on all reals are rejected, while branch-correct identities such as `root(x^3,3)=x` remain available.
- Source-shape replay is projected through one checked neutral-envelope rule rather than per-analyzer `+0` or `*1` exceptions. The projection reconstructs the builder's exact additive, multiplicative, or constant-fold operation from its raw operands, verifies its true guard and propagated definedness/continuity/differentiability formulas, requires the retained value term by reference, and preserves the original root guards and provenance. It is used only when the retained operand has the same definedness formula, so a value-zero term with a hidden source hole can never be discarded. Conservative regularity metadata on a total normalized affine value does not block an affine theorem whose premises require only exact value extraction and total definedness.
- Tucker, “A validated real function calculus,” and Dominik Gruntz’s limit framework are reserved specifications for a later validated analytic backend. The current engine does not convert an unimplemented analytic argument into a result.

Every published result crosses the independent `CertificateChecker`. Polynomial certificates replay their Sturm chains and cell signs. Theorem certificates re-derive the normalized obligation and result from the input semantic graph. Mutation or replay failure is converted to `Unknown(CertificateRejected)` by the production engine.
